01 — Program Evaluation

Does guaranteed income change outcomes for youth aging out of foster care?

County Government Agency
Guaranteed Income Pilot Evaluation
2020 – 2025
Longitudinal survey design Quantitative outcome analysis Digital storytelling Stakeholder coordination

Problem

Former foster youth face disproportionate risk of homelessness and financial instability once they age out of the system. The agency needed rigorous evidence on whether unconditional monthly cash payments — $1,200/month for two years — measurably changed housing, education, and employment outcomes, in order to decide whether to expand the program.

My Approach

  • Involved from project initiation, coordinating needs across the agency and partner-organization stakeholders
  • Defined scope and programmed a pre/post evaluation design: a baseline survey instrument and matched follow-up survey for a cohort of 122 former foster youth
  • Owned quantitative analysis and reporting across housing, education, employment, and financial-security outcome domains
  • Worked with participants to co-create digital stories (interactive video, poetry, photography) that made the quantitative findings tangible to funders and policymakers

Method note

Longitudinal pre/post design, n=122. Baseline survey fielded at enrollment; matched follow-up survey fielded roughly two years later, tracking the same respondents across eight quantitative outcome measures.

Impact: Findings contributed directly to the agency's decision to extend the program and fund it at a larger scale — a second cohort of former foster youth began payments the following year, and the model has since expanded into a nine-pilot regional guaranteed-income effort.
02 — Research Program Redesign

Building a research engine that kept HR leaders ahead of the trends that mattered.

National Research Association
Rapid-Cycle Research Program
Survey research Practitioner interviews Rapid-cycle studies Thought leadership

Problem

The organization's research process was built for long, infrequent reports — by the time a study shipped, the workforce and compensation trends it covered had often already moved on. Leadership needed research that HR leaders could act on while a trend was still current, not months later.

My Approach

I led the shift from occasional long-form reports to a repeatable model of bite-sized, quick-turnaround studies. For each study, I designed and fielded a structured quantitative survey and paired it with practitioner interviews, triangulating the two to move from raw data to a credible, publishable point of view fast. Run consistently, this became the organization's core thought-leadership engine on emerging HR and total-rewards topics.

Impact: The faster survey-plus-interview cadence let the organization publish credible research while a trend was still forming rather than after the fact, establishing its research team as a go-to source HR leaders turned to for what was current in their space — and building a repeatable model the organization continued to run studies on.
03 — Technical Product Management

Making a Bayesian marketing-mix model usable for the people who have to act on it.

Data & Analytics Consultancy
Marketing-Mix Measurement Platform
Client: Global Pharmaceutical Company
2023 – 2026
Bayesian modeling Technical PM Usability testing Statistical model QA

Problem

The client needed a single source of truth for marketing ROI across every channel — statistically rigorous enough for Data Science and Finance to trust, but interpretable enough for marketing teams to actually use in planning decisions. The platform uses Bayesian marketing-mix modeling to estimate incremental sales impact and ROI at the channel level from aggregated, geo-level time-series data.

My Approach

  • Served as overall technical PM, guiding the roadmap by combining data science fluency with UX judgment
  • Ran multiple usability sessions directly with the client's marketing teams — the platform's end users
  • Reviewed model outputs from both a statistical and a business/user perspective, and helped resolve underlying data issues
  • Guided decisions on modeling methodology, new features, and the specific data cuts and granularity the platform exposed

Quantitative approach

The model follows a Bayesian workflow: industry benchmarks and prior experiments set the prior, channel-level spend/impressions/reach/sales/competitor data form the evidence, and the two combine into a posterior, probability-weighted ROI estimate per channel. My role included pushing the model from national-aggregate estimation to geo-level (zip3) granularity, shifting the core exposure metric from raw impressions to reach and frequency, and moving validation from simple correlation to calibration against real lift tests — all reviewed jointly with Data Science and Finance before release.
